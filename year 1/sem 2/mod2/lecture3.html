Key Definitions

Terms to Know Cold

Financial market
A market where funds move from lender-savers to borrower-spenders through securities or other financial claims.
Security
A financial instrument or claim. It is an asset for the buyer and a liability for the issuer.
Direct finance
Borrowers raise funds directly from savers by selling securities in financial markets.
Indirect finance
Funds pass through a financial intermediary such as a bank, pension fund, insurer, or investment fund.
Primary market
The market for newly issued securities, where the issuer raises new finance.
Secondary market
The market where existing securities are resold between investors.
Broker
An agent who matches buyers and sellers without normally holding inventory.
Dealer
A market participant who buys and sells from inventory and earns the bid-ask spread.
Clearing house
An institution that checks trades, becomes counterparty to each side, and reduces default risk before settlement.
Margin account
A broker account that allows borrowing to buy securities, creating leverage and margin-call risk.
Maintenance margin
The minimum equity percentage that must be maintained in a margin account.
Continuously compounded rate
A rate quoted as if interest is compounded at every instant, using [[math:FV=PV e^{rt}]].
Core finance rule

The relevant return convention must match the compounding and cash-flow timing in the problem. A stated annual rate, an effective annual rate, and a continuously compounded rate can describe different accumulations even when the headline percentages look similar.

01

Compounding and Effective Rates

The lecture begins with a simple but examinable point: a quoted annual rate is not automatically the return you actually earn over a year. If a bank quotes 5 percent per year compounded semi-annually, the investor receives 2.5 percent after six months and then earns interest on the new balance for the next six months. That is more than a simple 5 percent annual return.

[[display:\frac{Effective annual rate = (1 + stated annual rate}{m)^{m} - 1}]]

Here, m is the number of compounding periods per year. With quarterly payments, m = 4; with monthly compounding, m = 12; with daily compounding, m is normally 365. As m rises, the effective annual rate rises for the same stated annual rate because interest is added to the account more frequently.

Rate conventionMeaningExam use
Annual stated rateA nominal yearly rate annualised using simple interest. It does not itself include the effect of compounding within the year.Convert it before comparing products with different compounding frequencies.
Effective annual rateThe actual one-year percentage growth after compounding is included.Use this to compare investments or loans on a like-for-like annual basis.
APRThe total cost of borrowing or investing as a percentage rate, including interest and fees where relevant.If there are no additional costs, APR and EAR may coincide; if there are fees, APR captures more than interest alone.
Continuous compoundingThe limiting case as compounding becomes infinitely frequent.Use [[math:FV=PV e^{rt}]], [[math:PV=FV e^{-rt}]], or [[math:r=\frac{\ln(FV/PV)}{t}]].
Exam insight

Do not compare a 13.7 percent annual rate with a 13.5 percent semi-annual rate by looking only at the headline number. Convert both to future values or effective annual rates first.

02

Financial Markets and Direct Finance

Financial markets transfer funds from lender-savers, who have surplus funds, to borrower-spenders, who need funds. Households, companies, and governments can appear on either side. A household might save through shares or pension contributions, but it might also borrow through a mortgage. A government may borrow through bonds while also investing temporary cash balances.

In direct finance, the borrower obtains funds by selling securities directly to savers in a market. The security is an asset to the saver because it gives a claim to future cash flows; the same security is a liability to the borrower because it creates an obligation.

Direct finance flow
  • Savers provide cash to borrowers through the financial market.
  • Borrowers issue financial claims such as shares, bonds, or other securities.
  • The market helps price those claims and lets investors decide whether the expected return compensates for the risk.

Why markets matter

The slides identify three core economic functions. First, markets provide information about the economy because prices react to expectations about cash flows, interest rates, inflation, and risk. Second, they allow transfer of consumption timing: savers defer consumption today, while borrowers bring future spending forward. Third, they allow risk to be reallocated to investors who are more willing or able to bear it.

03

Intermediaries and Indirect Finance

Indirect finance routes funds through financial intermediaries. Intermediaries are important because they reduce transaction costs, pool funds, screen borrowers, monitor risk, transform maturity, and often provide liquidity to savers who do not want to negotiate directly with borrowers.

IntermediaryMain roleLecture or seminar angle
Building societiesMutual institutions offering banking and financial services, especially residential mortgage lending.Nationwide is a typical example.
Retail banksTake deposits, make loans, provide payment services, and intermediate between savers and borrowers.Classic indirect finance institution.
Investment banksHelp firms and governments raise finance and arrange deals.Important in primary market issuance.
Finance housesFinance hire purchase, leasing, and other instalment credit.Connects markets to household and business credit.
Pension fundsCollect employer and employee contributions and invest to provide retirement income.Often hold conservative portfolios because they must meet long-term liabilities.
Insurance companiesCollect premiums, insure against financial hazards, and invest pooled funds.Risk pooling plus long-term investment.

Investment pool vehicles

Unit trusts are open-ended funds: units are bought and sold directly from fund managers, and there is no fixed limit on the number of units. Investment trusts are closed-ended companies that issue shares; after launch, investors trade those shares with other investors in the secondary market. Exchange traded funds are pooled vehicles that trade on exchanges like shares and often track an index or sector.

Evaluation

Intermediaries make finance easier, but they also add agency issues, fees, and potential systemic links. A strong answer says why intermediation improves allocation while recognising that it does not remove risk from the system.

04

Market Categories

Financial markets can be classified in several overlapping ways. These categories are useful because they explain who receives the money, how liquid the security is, what kind of risk is being traded, and how the market is organised.

CategoryDefinitionWhy it matters
Primary marketNew issues of securities are sold to initial buyers.The issuer raises new funds. Investment banks often assist the sale.
Secondary marketPreviously issued securities are resold between investors.Creates liquidity, supports price discovery, and makes primary issues more attractive.
ExchangeAn organised market with trading facilities and specific regulation.Trading is centralised and rules are clearer.
Over-the-counter marketA decentralised market with no single physical trading location.Trading is arranged through dealer networks or electronic communication.
Money marketShort-term, marketable, liquid, low-risk debt securities.Useful for short-term funding and cash management.
Capital marketLonger-term and generally riskier securities.Used for long-term finance and investment.

Another classification is by asset type: debt markets, share or equity markets, foreign exchange markets, and derivative markets. Debt markets focus on borrowing and fixed income claims. Equity markets give ownership claims. Foreign exchange markets trade currencies. Derivative markets trade contracts whose value depends on an underlying asset or rate.

Common mistake

Do not say secondary markets raise money for the issuing firm. The company receives funds in the primary issue. Secondary markets matter because investors are more willing to buy primary issues when they know they can resell later.

05

Exchanges, Orders and Spreads

Stock exchanges are organised markets where governments and companies can raise capital and investors can buy and sell securities. The London Stock Exchange began in the eighteenth century and now operates without a physical trading floor; finance houses are connected electronically and by communication systems. It supports both primary and secondary market activity.

The LSE includes the Main Market, for companies seeking the official list, and the Alternative Investment Market, which is more lightly regulated and designed for smaller or growing companies. Most modern transactions are electronic, and the largest equity market globally is the United States.

Trading conceptDefinitionInterpretation
Market orderAn order to buy or sell immediately at current market prices.Prioritises speed and execution certainty over price control.
Limit orderAn order to buy or sell only at a specified price or better.Prioritises price control but may not execute.
Bid priceThe price at which a dealer is willing to buy.An investor selling immediately receives the bid.
Ask priceThe price at which a dealer is willing to sell.An investor buying immediately pays the ask.
SpreadThe difference between the ask and bid prices.Compensates dealers for immediacy, inventory risk, and costs. Competition tends to narrow spreads.
Tick sizeThe minimum allowed price movement.In the UK example from the slides, this is 0.1p.
Seminar true or falseOrders

An investor who wishes to sell shares immediately should ask for a limit order; the ask price is less than the bid price.

Answer logic

Both statements are false. Immediate sale normally uses a market order, not a limit order. The ask price is normally above the bid price; otherwise dealers could not earn a positive spread.

06

Clearing, Settlement and Margin

After a trade is agreed, the exchange clears the trade. A clearing house checks the transaction and acts as counterparty, becoming the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer. This reduces counterparty default risk because each trader relies on the clearing system rather than only on the original trading partner.

Settlement is the completion of the transaction: securities and cash are transferred. Clearing and settlement are not decorative back-office details; they are what make market prices usable because traders trust that agreed trades will actually complete.

Cash accounts and margin accounts

A cash account works like a normal positive-balance account held with the broker. A margin account allows borrowing from the broker, so the investor can buy more securities than their own cash alone would permit. This creates leverage: gains and losses are magnified relative to the investor's own equity.

[[display:\frac{Margin = investor equity}{value of securities}]]
1
Find security value: number of shares x share price.
2
Find debt owed: broker loan plus any interest if the question asks for a later date.
3
Find investor equity: security value minus debt owed.
4
Compare with maintenance margin: if the margin is below the required level, there is a margin call.
Common mistake

When the question moves to the end of the year, include the interest owed on the broker loan. In Seminar 3, the loan is 4,000 at 8 percent, so the debt becomes 4,320 after one year.

07

Short Selling and Investment Funds

Short selling means borrowing shares, selling them now, and hoping to buy them back later at a lower price before returning them to the lender. The position profits when the share price falls. It loses when the share price rises, and the potential loss can be very large because there is no fixed upper bound on the share price.

Hedge funds are investment funds that try to generate positive returns from manager skill rather than simply from general market movements. The slides note that hedge funds are usually open only to wealthy or institutional investors and may use strategies not normally used by conventional funds, including short selling.

PositionProfit whenMain risk
Long positionThe asset price rises.The price can fall, with loss limited to the invested amount if no borrowing is used.
Short positionThe asset price falls.The price can rise, creating potentially very large losses and margin pressure.
Leveraged longThe asset price rises enough to cover borrowing cost.Losses are magnified and may trigger a margin call.
Evaluation

Short selling can improve price discovery by allowing negative information to enter prices, but it also increases sensitivity to liquidity, borrowing constraints, and margin requirements. In answers, separate the trading mechanism from the judgement about whether it is socially useful.

08

Indices and Market Information

Equity indices summarise movements in groups of shares. They are useful reference points for investors because they convert many individual price changes into a single market signal. The lecture lists FTSE 100, FTSE 250, FTSE 350, FTSE SmallCap, FTSE All-Share, FTSE Fledgling, and FTSE AIM All-Share. Similar roles are played internationally by indices such as the Dow Jones, S&P 500, and Nikkei.

IndexMeaningHow to use in an answer
FTSE 100Tracks the largest UK-listed companies by market capitalisation.A large-cap benchmark, but not a full picture of every UK listed company.
FTSE All-ShareA broader UK equity index covering a much larger share of listed UK market value.Better for discussing the wider listed equity market.
FTSE FledglingContains smaller companies not large enough for the main larger-company indices.Useful when discussing smaller listed firms and different liquidity/risk profiles.

For graph discussion, move beyond describing up and down movement. Link index changes to likely information: expected earnings, macroeconomic conditions, interest rates, inflation expectations, risk appetite, and shocks such as the Covid period covered by the Seminar 3 FTSE prompt.

09

Seminar Calculations

Margin account worked structure

Seminar 3 asks about buying 300 shares at 40 dollars each while borrowing 4,000 dollars from the broker at 8 percent. Initial security value is 300 x 40 = 12,000. Initial equity is 12,000 - 4,000 = 8,000. Initial margin is therefore 8,000 / 12,000 = 66.67 percent.

If the share price falls to 30 dollars by the end of the year, security value becomes 300 x 30 = 9,000. The loan has grown to 4,000 x 1.08 = 4,320. Equity is 9,000 - 4,320 = 4,680, so margin is 4,680 / 9,000 = 52 percent. Because 52 percent is above the 30 percent maintenance margin, there is no margin call.

Continuously compounded rate

For a deposit of 422.41 growing to 1,000 in 10 years, use the continuous compounding rearrangement:

[[display:\frac{r = ln(FV}{PV) / t = ln(1000 / 422.41) / 10 = approximately 8.62 percent}]]

Semi-annual lease payment logic

If cash flows arrive every six months and the annual discount rate is 9 percent, the six-month rate is 4.5 percent when the problem treats the stated annual rate as proportional. The immediate payment is already at time zero, so it is not discounted. The nine later payments are discounted one half-year, two half-years, and so on.

[[display:PV = 101,000 + 101,000/1.045 + 101,000/1.045^{2} + ... + 101,000/1.045^{9}]]

This gives a present value of approximately 835,147.84 dollars.

10

Exam Prompts and Common Mistakes

Assessment-style prompt8 marks

Why are financial markets important?

Model answer plan

Define financial markets, then explain capital allocation, price discovery, liquidity, consumption timing, and risk transfer. Add that secondary market liquidity supports primary market funding because investors are more willing to buy new securities when resale is possible.

Assessment-style prompt10 marks

Distinguish direct finance from indirect finance and explain the role of financial intermediaries.

Model answer plan

Direct finance involves borrowers selling securities directly to savers. Indirect finance uses institutions such as banks, pension funds, insurers, and investment funds. Explain how intermediaries pool funds, reduce transaction and information costs, transform maturity, monitor risk, and provide liquidity.

Assessment-style prompt10 marks

A stated annual rate is 8 percent compounded quarterly. Explain how to find the effective annual rate and why it differs from 8 percent.

Model answer plan

Use [[math:EAR=(1+0.08/4)^4-1]]. It differs from 8 percent because interest is credited each quarter and later quarters earn interest on earlier interest. The EAR is approximately 8.24 percent.

Evaluation checklist

For institution questions, connect the definition to a function: liquidity, information, risk sharing, funding, or settlement. For calculation questions, state the timing convention before using the formula. For market-structure questions, separate who receives new money from who trades an existing claim.

Common mistakes

Do not confuse bid and ask; the dealer buys at the bid and sells at the ask. Do not discount an immediate cash flow. Do not ignore loan interest in margin calculations. Do not treat APR, stated annual rate, EAR, and continuous compounding as interchangeable labels.
