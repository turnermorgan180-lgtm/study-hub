Question 1

Clearing houses

Full seminar question

What are clearing houses and how do they reduce the risk to investors?

Model answerSeminar

A clearing house stands between counterparties and becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer. That reduces counterparty risk because settlement no longer depends purely on the credit quality of the original trading partner.

They also impose margin and netting arrangements, which lower the chance that one default triggers wider market disruption.

Question 2

Short selling

Full seminar question

Explain what is short selling.

Model answerSeminar

Short selling means borrowing a security, selling it now, and hoping to buy it back later at a lower price so the borrowed security can be returned.

The position profits when price falls and loses when price rises, which is why the downside risk can be very large.

Question 3

Margin account

Full seminar question

A trader opens an account with a broker and buys 300 shares of a stock valued at $40 per share. She borrows $4000 from her broker. The interest rate on the loan is 8%. What is the margin in the account at the moment of investment? If the share price falls to $30 per share by the end of the year, what is the margin on the account? If the maintenance margin is 30%, will she receive a margin call?

Model answerSeminar

Initial position value is 300 x 40 = 12,000. Equity is 12,000 - 4,000 = 8,000, so initial margin is 66.67 percent.

After one year at 30 per share, asset value is 9,000. Loan owed is 4,000 x 1.08 = 4,320, so equity is 4,680 and margin is 52 percent.

Because 52 percent is above the 30 percent maintenance margin, there is no margin call.

Question 4

Orders and bid-ask

Full seminar question

Are the following statements true or false: an investor who wishes to sell shares immediately should ask his broker to enter a limit order; the ask price is less than the bid price.

Model answerSeminar

The first statement is false. An investor who wants to sell immediately normally uses a market order rather than a limit order.

The second statement is false. The ask price is normally above the bid price; the difference is the bid-ask spread.

Question 5

Pension funds

Full seminar question

Pension funds tend to have portfolios heavily dependent on low-risk investments like government bonds. Why do you think that pension funds have conservative portfolios of investments?

Model answerSeminar

Pension funds have long-dated, promised liabilities. Because they must meet payments to retirees, they favour assets with relatively stable cash flows and lower downside risk.

The central logic is asset-liability matching rather than simple return maximisation.

Question 6

Financial intermediaries

Full seminar question

Identify the different types of financial intermediaries.

Model answerSeminar

Key intermediaries include commercial banks, investment banks, insurance companies, pension funds, mutual funds, hedge funds, building societies, finance houses, and brokers or dealers operating in market-making roles.

The common function is channeling funds from savers to borrowers while reducing information, transaction, and liquidity frictions.

Question 7

FTSE indices

Full seminar question

Explain the following: FTSE100; FTSE Allshare; FTSE Fledgling.

Model answerSeminar

The FTSE100 tracks the largest listed UK companies by market capitalisation. The FTSE All-Share is broader and captures a much larger share of the UK equity market.

The FTSE Fledgling contains smaller listed companies not large enough for the main large-cap and mid-cap indices.

Question 9

Importance of markets

Full seminar question

Why are financial markets important?

Model answerSeminar

Financial markets allocate capital, produce prices, improve liquidity, share risk, and help firms and households access funding.

In macro terms they support investment and growth. In micro terms they let investors rebalance, diversify, and value claims continuously.

Question 10

Brokers vs dealers

Full seminar question

Distinguish brokers from dealers.

Model answerSeminar

A broker matches buyers and sellers and earns commission. A dealer trades from inventory and earns the spread between buying and selling prices.

This distinction matters because dealers provide immediacy while brokers primarily provide matching and execution.

Question 11

Continuous compounding

Full seminar question

A deposit of £422.41 will grow into a future value of £1000 in 10 years. What is the continuously compounded rate?

Model answerSeminar

If 422.41 grows to 1,000 in 10 years, the continuously compounded rate is ln(1000 / 422.41) / 10 = about 8.62 percent.

Question 12

Lease PV

Full seminar question

A leasing contract calls for an immediate payment of $101,000 and nine subsequent $101,000 semi-annual payments at six-month intervals. What is the PV of these payments if the annual discount rate is 9%?

Model answerSeminar

Use the six-month discount rate of 4.5 percent. Present value equals the immediate 101,000 plus the PV of nine further 101,000 payments discounted at 4.5 percent each half-year.

That gives a total PV of about 835,147.84.

Question 13

Compounding comparison

Full seminar question

Calculate the future value of a £1 investment paying interest of 13.7% compounded annually after 1, 5, and 20 years. Do the same for 13.5% compounded semi-annually and 13.3% compounded continuously. Which investment would you prefer?

Model answerSeminar

For annual compounding at 13.7 percent, GBP1 grows to about 1.137 after 1 year, 1.900 after 5 years, and 13.038 after 20 years.

For 13.5 percent stated with semi-annual compounding, values are about 1.140, 1.922, and 13.637. For 13.3 percent continuously compounded, values are about 1.142, 1.944, and 14.296.

The continuously compounded option is best here because it gives the highest effective accumulation.

Question 14

Annual vs continuous 16%

Full seminar question

How much will you have at the end of 15 years if you invest £500 today at 16% annually compounded? How much will you have if you invest at 16% continuously compounded?

Model answerSeminar

With annual compounding, future value is about 4,632.76.

With continuous compounding, future value is about 5,511.59, so continuous compounding again produces the larger amount.
