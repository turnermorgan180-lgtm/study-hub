Question 1

Stocks-bonds portfolio

Full seminar question

Consider the following scenario analysis: recession probability 0.20 with stocks -5% and bonds 14%; normal economy probability 0.60 with stocks 15% and bonds 8%; boom probability 0.20 with stocks 25% and bonds 4%. Consider a portfolio with weights of 0.6 in stocks and 0.4 in bonds. What is the portfolio return in each scenario? What are its expected return and standard deviation? Would you prefer the portfolio, stocks only, or bonds only?

Model answerSeminar

Portfolio returns by state are 2.6 percent in recession, 12.2 percent in a normal economy, and 16.6 percent in a boom.

Expected portfolio return is 11.16 percent and standard deviation is about 4.61 percent. This sits between the risk and return of the two individual assets, but diversification lowers risk materially.

Question 2

Diversification logic

Full seminar question

Explain why diversification reduces the total risk of a portfolio.

Model answerSeminar

Diversification works because asset returns are not perfectly positively correlated. When one asset performs badly, another may hold up or even perform well, reducing overall portfolio volatility.

The crucial point is that diversification mainly removes firm-specific risk, not market-wide risk.

Question 3

Systematic vs unsystematic

Full seminar question

Discuss the two types of risk and give examples of each type of risk.

Model answerSeminar

Unsystematic risk is asset-specific or firm-specific risk, such as a factory fire or product recall. It can largely be diversified away.

Systematic risk is economy-wide risk, such as inflation shocks, recessions, or rate changes. Because it affects many assets together, it cannot be diversified away completely.

Question 4

Two-stock portfolio

Full seminar question

Two shares, XYZ and HGJ, are expected to generate the following returns with probabilities 0.32, 0.40, and 0.28. XYZ returns are -5%, 10%, and 5%; HGJ returns are 20%, 2%, and -1%. Calculate expected returns and standard deviations for both shares, then for a 50/50 portfolio. Comment on why the portfolio standard deviation is not the average of the two individual standard deviations. Knowing that the correlation is -0.89643, estimate the portfolio standard deviation using the alternative method.

Model answerSeminar

Expected return is 3.8 percent for XYZ and 6.92 percent for HGJ. Standard deviations are about 6.37 percent and 9.06 percent respectively.

A 50-50 portfolio has expected return 5.36 percent and standard deviation about 2.19 percent. That risk is much lower than the average of the individual standard deviations because correlation is strongly negative.

Using the covariance formula with correlation minus 0.89643 gives essentially the same portfolio standard deviation, which is a useful consistency check.

Question 5

Beta definition

Full seminar question

What is the beta of a security?

Model answerSeminar

Beta measures how sensitive a security’s return is to movements in the market portfolio. It is a measure of systematic risk, not total risk.

A beta above one means the asset tends to move more than the market; a beta below one means less than the market.

Question 6

High beta meaning

Full seminar question

A security that has a beta of 2 is more or less affected than the market to changes in macro variables like inflation?

Model answerSeminar

A security with beta 2 is more affected than the market by macroeconomic shocks. In broad terms, if market returns move because of economy-wide news, the security tends to move about twice as much on average.

Question 7

CAPM calculations

Full seminar question

Suppose the risk-free rate is 7%. The expected return on the market is 17%. According to the CAPM what is the expected return of a share with a beta of 1.3? If another share has a return of 25% what should its beta be?

Model answerSeminar

Expected return on a stock with beta 1.3 is 7 percent + 1.3 x 10 percent = 20 percent.

If another share has expected return 25 percent, its beta should be (25 - 7) / 10 = 1.8.

Question 8

Expected return with beta 1.2

Full seminar question

Assuming that the CAPM holds, what is the expected return of Overthemoon plc shares? Overthemoon has systematic risk of 1.2, the expected market return is 5%, and risk-free assets currently pay 0.5%.

Model answerSeminar

Using CAPM, expected return is 0.5 percent + 1.2 x (5 percent - 0.5 percent) = 5.9 percent.

Question 9

CAPM comparison and portfolio beta

Full seminar question

Assume CAPM holds, the risk-free rate is 0.5% and the market risk premium 4%. Share A has beta 1.3 and share B beta 0.9. What are their expected returns? Is share A riskier than share B? A portfolio allocates 30% to A and 70% to B. What is its beta and expected return? If standard deviation of a portfolio is not the weighted average of component standard deviations, why is beta the weighted average of component betas?

Model answerSeminar

Share A expected return is 5.7 percent and Share B expected return is 4.1 percent, using a 0.5 percent risk-free rate and 4 percent market risk premium.

Share A is riskier in systematic terms because it has the higher beta. A portfolio with 30 percent in A and 70 percent in B has beta 1.02 and expected return 4.58 percent.

Portfolio beta is a weighted average because beta is a linear covariance-based measure with the market, unlike standard deviation, which depends on covariance between portfolio components.

Question 10

Beta equal to market

Full seminar question

Assuming CAPM holds, what is the expected return on a share which has the same beta as the market portfolio? The risk-free return is 4% and the market risk premium has a return of 10%.

Model answerSeminar

A share with the same beta as the market has beta 1, so expected return is 4 percent + 10 percent = 14 percent.

Question 11

MCQ on CAPM

Full seminar question

Which of the following statements is correct: a security with beta zero will provide expected return of zero; the Security Market Line shows the relation between expected return and beta; investors demand higher expected returns on stocks they do not like; an investor who puts £20,000 in gilts and £15,000 in the market portfolio will have portfolio beta of 2; none of the above.

Model answerSeminar

The correct statement is that the Security Market Line shows the relationship between expected return and beta.

The others are wrong because a beta of zero should earn the risk-free rate, dislike alone does not determine expected return, and the given mixed portfolio beta is nowhere near 2.

Question 12

Expected return, SD, and beta of 70-30 portfolio

Full seminar question

There are 2 securities in the market, A and B. The price of A today is £50; next year the price will be £40 if a recession hits, £55 if the economy is normal, and £60 if the economy is in a boom. The probabilities are 0.1, 0.8, and 0.1. A pays no dividends and has correlation 0.8 with the market portfolio. B also pays no dividends, has expected return of 9%, standard deviation of 12%, correlation with the market of 0.2, and correlation with A of 0.6. The market portfolio standard deviation is 10%. What are the expected return and standard deviation of a portfolio consisting of 70% of A and 30% of B? What is the beta of the portfolio?

Model answerSeminar

Security A has expected return of 8 percent and standard deviation about 9.80 percent, based on the recession, normal, and boom payoffs from a current price of 50.

With 70 percent in A and 30 percent in B, portfolio expected return is 8.3 percent and standard deviation about 9.47 percent.

Beta of A is 0.8 x 9.80 / 10 = about 0.784. Beta of B is 0.2 x 12 / 10 = 0.24. Portfolio beta is therefore about 0.621.
