Seminar focus

Question 1

Debt vs equity

Full seminar question

What are the relative advantages and drawbacks of debt and equity finance?

Model answerSeminar

Debt is usually cheaper, preserves control, and interest may be tax deductible. Its drawback is fixed payment obligation and greater financial distress risk.

Equity is more flexible because dividends are not mandatory and repayment is not fixed, but it is generally more expensive and may dilute control.

Question 2

Bond terminology

Full seminar question

Explain the following related to bonds: par value; debenture; zero coupon rate; floating-rate bonds.

Model answerSeminar

Par value is the amount repaid at maturity. A debenture is unsecured or supported by general credit rather than a specific coupon formula, depending on jurisdictional usage, so definitions should be stated carefully.

A zero-coupon bond pays no periodic coupon and is sold at a discount. A floating-rate bond has coupons that reset with a reference rate instead of staying fixed.

Question 3

Restrictive covenants

Full seminar question

Why do bond issuers accept restrictive covenants?

Model answerSeminar

Covenants reassure lenders that the borrower will not take actions that undermine debt repayment, such as excessive new borrowing or asset stripping.

Because they reduce lender risk, covenants can lower the yield investors demand and make issuance easier.

Question 4

Consol pricing

Full seminar question

Perpetual Life Corp. has issued consol bonds with coupon payments of $60 and face value of $1000. If the required rate of return was 6%, at what price were they sold? If the required return today is 10%, at what price do the consols sell?

Model answerSeminar

A consol paying 60 forever is worth 60 / 0.06 = 1,000 when required return is 6 percent.

If required return rises to 10 percent, price falls to 60 / 0.10 = 600. This neatly shows the inverse price-yield relationship.

Question 5

Implied maturity

Full seminar question

Sure Tea Co. has issued 9% annual coupon bonds that are now selling at an annual yield to maturity of 10% and a price of $914.87 with face value of $1000. What is the remaining maturity of these bonds?

Model answerSeminar

Set 914.87 equal to the present value of a 9 percent annual coupon bond discounted at 10 percent. Solving by trial and interpolation gives a maturity of about 20 years remaining.

The key logic is that a discount bond with coupon below required return must have a price below par.

Question 6

20-year annual bond

Full seminar question

What is the price of a 20-year 7% coupon bond with annual interest payments, face value £100, and annual yield to maturity of 6%?

Model answerSeminar

Price = 7 x annuity factor at 6 percent for 20 years + 100 discounted for 20 years. That gives about 111.47.

Because yield is below the coupon rate, the bond sells at a premium.

Question 7

Semi-annual bond price

Full seminar question

What is the price of a 10-year 7% coupon bond with semi-annual interest payments, face value £100, and annual yield to maturity of 6%?

Model answerSeminar

Use 20 half-year periods, coupon of 3.5 each half-year, and discount rate of 3 percent per half-year.

The resulting price is about 107.44, again a premium because the coupon rate exceeds the required yield.

Question 8

Multi-stage bond pricing

Full seminar question

Company X has two bonds outstanding. Bond X has face value £20,000 and matures in 20 years. It makes no payments for the first 6 years, then £1,200 every 6 months over the next 8 years, and finally £1,500 every 6 months over the last 6 years. Bond Z also has face value £20,000 and maturity 20 years but makes no coupon payments. If required return on both is 10% per year compounded semi-annually, what is the current price of these bonds?

Model answerSeminar

Bond X is priced by discounting each semi-annual cash flow separately: no coupons for 6 years, then 1,200 every six months for 8 years, then 1,500 every six months for the last 6 years, plus 20,000 at maturity.

Using a 5 percent semi-annual discount rate, Bond X is worth about 13,474.20. Bond Z, being a pure zero-coupon bond, is worth 20,000 discounted for 40 half-years, which is about 2,840.91.

Question 9

Yield changes and bond variables

Full seminar question

A 30-year Treasury bond is issued with face value of $1,000, paying interest of $60 per year. If market yields increase shortly after the bond is issued, what happens to the bond’s coupon rate, price, yield to maturity, and current yield?

Model answerSeminar

Coupon rate does not change because coupon payments are fixed by contract at issue.

Price falls, yield to maturity rises, and current yield rises because the same coupon is divided by a lower market price.

Question 10

Discount bond interpretation

Full seminar question

If a bond with face value of $1,000 and coupon rate of 8% is selling at a price of $970, is the bond’s yield to maturity more or less than 8%? What about the current yield?

Model answerSeminar

Yield to maturity must be above 8 percent because if YTM equalled coupon rate the bond would sell at par.

Current yield is also above 8 percent because coupon payment of 80 divided by 970 is about 8.25 percent.

Question 11

AT&T bond yields

Full seminar question

An AT&T bond has 10 years until maturity, a $1,000 face value, a coupon rate of 8%, and sells for $1,100. What is the current yield? What is the yield to maturity?

Model answerSeminar

Current yield is 80 / 1,100 = 7.27 percent.

Yield to maturity is lower than the coupon rate because the bond sells at a premium. Solving the pricing equation gives a YTM of about 6.60 percent.

Question 12

Bond HPR

Full seminar question

What is the one-year holding-period return of a bond which pays an annual coupon of 5% and is today being sold for £110 while last year’s price was £105? Face value is £100.

Model answerSeminar

Cash return is coupon plus price change = 5 + (110 - 105) = 10.

Percentage holding-period return is 10 / 105 = about 9.52 percent. This differs from yield to maturity because HPR is the realised one-year return over the holding period, while YTM is the internal rate if held to maturity.

Question 13

Semi-annual YTM

Full seminar question

A bond which matures in 10 years pays a semi-annual coupon, the annual coupon rate is 10%, and the bond is being sold today at £95 with par value £100. What is the annual yield to maturity?

Model answerSeminar

Set price equal to the PV of 20 half-year coupons of 5 plus redemption of 100. Solving gives a half-year yield of about 5.42 percent.

The annual proportional YTM is therefore about 10.83 percent.

Question 14

Credit ratings

Full seminar question

Explain what bond credit ratings are and what are the 2 main factors which contribute to the credit rating of bonds.

Model answerSeminar

Bond credit ratings are external assessments of credit quality, with higher ratings indicating lower expected default risk.

The two major determinants are probability of default and loss severity or protective covenant structure. Strong covenants and strong issuer cash flow support higher ratings.

Question 15

Gilt one-year return

Full seminar question

The UK Government issues a five-year bond which makes annual coupon payments of 5% and offers a yield of 3% annually compounded. Suppose that one year later the bond still yields 3%. What return has the bondholder earned over the 12-month period? Now suppose that the bond yields 2% at the end of the year. What return will the bondholder get in this case?

Model answerSeminar

If yield stays at 3 percent, the one-year holding-period return is 3 percent because nothing unexpected happens to pricing.

If yield falls to 2 percent, price rises and the bondholder earns a capital gain on top of the coupon, so the holding-period return increases to about 6.65 percent.
