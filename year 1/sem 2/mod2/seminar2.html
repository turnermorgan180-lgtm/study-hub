Question 1

Perpetuity rate

Full seminar question

A property will provide $10,000 a year forever. If its value is $125,000, what must be the discount rate?

Model answerSeminar

If a property pays 10,000 forever and is worth 125,000, the discount rate is C / PV = 10,000 / 125,000 = 8 percent.

Question 2

Growing perpetuity PV

Full seminar question

What is the present value of a perpetuity of annual payment if the interest rate is 4%? The first payment is £150 and thereafter the payments grow at an annual rate of 1.5% forever.

Model answerSeminar

For a growing perpetuity, PV = C1 / (r - g). Here PV = 150 / (0.04 - 0.015) = 6,000.

The key condition is that the discount rate exceeds the growth rate.

Question 3

Lottery payoff choice

Full seminar question

Suppose you just won the lottery and must choose one of the following guaranteed payoffs. Which one would you choose if the interest rate is 5% per year? £80,000 paid today; £110,000 paid five years from today; £50,000 paid today and £40,000 paid four years from today; £17,000 paid per year during 6 years with the first payment made today.

Model answerSeminar

Bring all alternatives to time 0. The present values are approximately: 80,000 today = 80,000; 110,000 in five years = 86,185; 50,000 today plus 40,000 in year four = 82,908; 17,000 for six years starting today = 90,601.

The annuity-due option is therefore best because it has the highest present value.

Question 4

Mortgage payment

Full seminar question

Mr. and Mrs. Palin want to buy a house worth £200,000. They have £50,000 for the initial payment and will borrow the remaining amount over 30 years with end-of-month payments and a mortgage rate of 0.5% per month. How much must they pay per month?

Model answerSeminar

Borrowed amount is 150,000 after the 50,000 deposit. With a monthly rate of 0.5 percent and 360 end-of-month payments, the monthly payment is about GBP899.33.

This is an annuity-immediate because the first mortgage payment is one month after origination.

Question 5

Instalment plan

Full seminar question

A store offers 2 payment plans. Under the instalment plan, you pay 25% down and 25% of the purchase price in each of the next 3 years. If you pay the entire bill immediately, you can take a 10% discount. Which is better if you can borrow or lend at 5%?

Model answerSeminar

If the list price is 100, the instalment plan has PV of about 93.08, while paying immediately with the discount costs 90.

The immediate payment is the better deal because its present value cost is lower.

Question 6

Loan repayments

Full seminar question

If you borrow $1,000 and agree to repay the loan in 5 equal annual payments at an interest rate of 12%, what will your payment be? What if you make the first payment immediately instead of at the end of the first year?

Model answerSeminar

With five equal end-of-year payments, the annual payment is about 277.41.

If the first payment is immediate, the contract becomes an annuity due and the payment falls to about 247.69 because the lender receives cash sooner.

Question 7

Retirement saving

Full seminar question

You believe you will need to have saved $500,000 by the time you retire in 40 years. If the interest rate is 6% per year, how much must you save each year?

Model answerSeminar

To accumulate 500,000 in 40 years at 6 percent, required annual saving is about 3,230.77.

This is a future-value-of-annuity problem, so the target amount must be built up rather than discounted back from retirement.

Question 8

Retirement plus college

Full seminar question

A couple will retire in 50 years and plan to spend about $30,000 a year in retirement for 25 years. They can earn 8%. If they make annual payments into a savings plan, how much must they save each year? How does the answer change if in 20 years they also need $60,000 for a child’s college education?

Model answerSeminar

The retirement consumption stream has a value at retirement of about 320,243.29. Saving for that over 50 years at 8 percent requires roughly 558.14 per year.

If the couple also needs 60,000 in year 20 for college, required annual saving rises to about 1,610.41 because the extra liability must be funded as well.

Question 9

Mortgage balance

Full seminar question

You take out a 30-year £200,000 mortgage loan with monthly payments and a 0.5% monthly interest rate. Fourteen years later you decide to sell your house and pay off the mortgage. What is the balance on the loan?

Model answerSeminar

On a 200,000, 30-year mortgage at 0.5 percent per month, the balance after 168 payments is about GBP110,831.13.

The correct method is outstanding-balance valuation, not simply subtracting principal from total payments made.

Question 10

Effective annual rate

Full seminar question

If the stated annual proportional rate is 14% and compounding takes place monthly, what is the effective annual rate of interest?

Model answerSeminar

EAR = (1 + 0.14 / 12)^12 - 1 = about 14.93 percent.

The EAR exceeds the stated APR because of interest-on-interest within the year.

Question 11

Quarterly compounding

Full seminar question

How much must be invested now to provide £10,000 in 6 years assuming interest is compounded quarterly at a stated rate of 8%? What is the effective annual rate?

Model answerSeminar

Present value is 10,000 / (1 + 0.08 / 4)^24 = about 6,217.21.

The effective annual rate is (1.02)^4 - 1 = about 8.24 percent.

Question 12

Two-year perpetuity

Full seminar question

You have invested in a project that will pay you £1000 every 2 years forever. If the interest rate is 8% a year how much is the project worth?

Model answerSeminar

Convert the annual 8 percent rate into a two-year effective rate: (1.08)^2 - 1 = 16.64 percent.

The project value is then 1,000 / 0.1664 = about 6,009.62.

Question 13

Delayed growing perpetuity

Full seminar question

Company X expects to start selling a new product in 2 years. It expects to sell 2000 units a year at £200 per unit, with the first cash flow received 2 years from now. Annual cash flows then grow at 5% in perpetuity. What is the present value if the discount rate is 10%?

Model answerSeminar

Year-2 cash flow is 2,000 x 200 = 400,000. Value at year 1 is 400,000 / (0.10 - 0.05) = 8,000,000.

Discounting one year back gives a present value of about 7,272,727.27. The mark-scoring point is that a growing perpetuity formula gives value one period before the first cash flow.

Question 14

Changing-rate loan

Full seminar question

You negotiate a bank loan of £50,000 for 6 years. During the first 2 years the annual interest rate is 4%, and from the end of year 2 onwards the interest rate is 5%. If you agree to repay through constant annual end-of-year payments, what is the value of the payments?

Model answerSeminar

The first two years discount at 4 percent and later cash flows discount using both the initial 4 percent stage and the later 5 percent stage. The constant payment is found by equating the present value of six payments to 50,000.

A first-class answer sets up the timeline explicitly. Even if calculator precision differs slightly, the correct structure is what earns the marks.
