Question 1

Convertible metrics

Full seminar question

Pinto plc has issued £50m 15-year 8.0% coupon bonds with a nominal value of £100. Each bond is convertible into 30 Pinto shares, currently traded at £1.5. What is the conversion price, the conversion premium, and the conversion value of the bond?

Model answerSeminar

Conversion price is face value divided by conversion ratio: 100 / 30 = GBP3.33 per share.

Conversion value is 30 x 1.5 = GBP45. Conversion premium is (100 - 45) / 45 = about 122.22 percent, which shows the bond is far out of the money on conversion at current share price.

Question 2

Convertible bonds explained

Full seminar question

Explain what convertible bonds are, their main features, and why convertible bonds can be considered a hybrid instrument.

Model answerSeminar

A convertible bond begins life as debt: it pays coupons, has a maturity date, and ranks ahead of ordinary shareholders on insolvency.

It is a hybrid because the investor also has the option to convert into equity, so the instrument contains both bond-like downside protection and share-like upside potential.

Question 3

Downgrade pricing

Full seminar question

A bond’s credit rating provides a guide to its price. In December 2020, Aaa bonds yielded 5.3% and Baa bonds yielded 6.3%. Some bad news causes a 10% five-year bond to be unexpectedly downgraded from Aaa to Baa. Assume face value of £1000. What is the bond price if the bond is rated as Baa?

Model answerSeminar

After the downgrade, the relevant discount rate is 6.3 percent rather than 5.3 percent. Pricing a five-year 10 percent bond at 6.3 percent gives a value of about 1,154.59 for 1,000 face value.

The bond price falls when required yield rises, even if the coupon has not changed.

Question 4

Bond price path and return

Full seminar question

An 8.53% twenty-year bond yields 6.5%. If this yield to maturity remains unchanged, what will be its price now and in one year? Assume annual coupon payments and face value of £100. Refer back to part a. What is the total return to an investor who held the bond over this year?

Model answerSeminar

With coupon 8.53, YTM 6.5 percent, and face value 100, the current price is about 122.37.

If yield is unchanged next year, the price with 19 years remaining is about 121.79. Including the 8.53 coupon, total one-year return is about 6.5 percent, which matches the unchanged YTM intuition.

Question 5

Treasury pricing

Full seminar question

In November 2020, Treasury 4 3/4 of 2043 offered a semi-annually compounded yield to maturity of 2.72% annual rate. Recognizing that coupons are paid semi-annually, calculate the bond’s price. Assume face value is 100.

Model answerSeminar

Interpreting the bond as a 4.75 percent coupon, the semi-annual coupon is 2.375 and the semi-annual discount rate is 1.36 percent.

Discounting 46 half-year coupons plus redemption gives a price of about 134.54. The premium price reflects coupon far above yield.

Question 6

Lease or buy

Full seminar question

Your landscaping company can lease a truck for $8,000 a year paid at year-end for 6 years. It can instead buy the truck for $40,000. The truck will be valueless after 6 years. If the interest rate is 7%, is it cheaper to buy or lease?

Model answerSeminar

The present value of leasing at 8,000 for six years discounted at 7 percent is about 38,132.32.

Because buying costs 40,000 today and the truck is valueless after six years, leasing is slightly cheaper on present-value grounds.

Question 7

Short-term finance sources

Full seminar question

Briefly explain the main sources of short and medium term finance discussed in the module: overdraft; trade credit; factoring; bills of exchange; acceptance credits; hire purchase and leasing. Discuss also what the advantages of leasing over hire purchase agreements are.

Model answerSeminar

Overdraft provides flexible short-term borrowing but can be expensive and uncertain. Trade credit delays payment to suppliers but may strain relationships if overused. Factoring improves cash flow by selling receivables, often at a cost.

Bills of exchange and acceptance credits formalise trade-related payment promises. Hire purchase spreads the cost of an asset while transferring ownership later. Leasing provides use without immediate ownership and often preserves flexibility, which is a key advantage over hire purchase if technology or asset needs may change.

Question 8

Overdraft versus loan

Full seminar question

Financeme plc is trying to decide if it should request an overdraft or a loan to finance its cash position over 1 year. The firm needs 5 million over the first 9 months and an extra 5 million thereafter until the end of the year. Options available: loan of 10 million for 1 year at an effective annual rate of 8% with excess deposits earning 6%; overdraft of 10 million for 1 year with quarterly payment of interest of 2% per quarter. How should Financeme plc finance its cash positions? Show calculations.

Model answerSeminar

The one-year loan costs 10 million x 8 percent = 800,000 interest, but the unused 5 million during the first nine months can be deposited at 6 percent for about 225,000. Net financing cost is therefore about 575,000.

The overdraft costs 2 percent each quarter on the amount drawn. That gives 300,000 over the first three quarters on 5 million and 200,000 in the final quarter on 10 million, for about 500,000 total. The overdraft is therefore cheaper.

Question 9

Car credit APR

Full seminar question

John just bought a new car priced at £30,000 and agreed to pay £1,133.33 per month over the next 3 years at the end of each month. In addition to the monthly payments the dealer charges an admin fee of £250 up front. What APR is the dealer charging?

Model answerSeminar

Set the car price of 30,000 equal to the 250 upfront fee plus the present value of 36 monthly payments of 1,133.33. Solving gives a monthly rate of about 1.818 percent.

Converting that to an effective annual rate gives about 24.13 percent, which is very expensive consumer finance.
