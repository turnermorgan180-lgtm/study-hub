Core finance rule

Growth in simple settings is often written as g = retention rate × ROE.

Part 01

From Dividends to Price Multiples

Once students understand the dividend discount model, equity analysis expands to P/E ratios, payout, plowback, and return on equity. The stronger answer explains how these measures fit together rather than listing them separately.

Part 02

Plowback and Growth

Growth depends on how much earnings are retained and how productively those retained earnings are invested. Seminar material repeatedly uses the relation between payout ratio, plowback ratio, ROE, and dividend growth.

Part 03

P/E Ratios and Interpretation

A P/E ratio is not just a market statistic. It embeds assumptions about discount rates, growth, and expected profitability. If risk falls or growth prospects rise, P/E ratios can rise even if current earnings do not.

Part 04

Valuing Growth Opportunities

Seminar questions emphasise the difference between a firm with no new investments and one with positive-NPV growth opportunities. This is important because share price can be decomposed into current earnings power plus the value of growth opportunities.

Part 05

Multi-Stage Equity Valuation

Not all firms have one stable growth rate forever. A better answer explains when multi-stage growth is more realistic and how the valuation is stitched together across stages.

Part 06

Exam Framing

Do not treat higher retention as automatically good. Retained earnings create value only if reinvested at attractive returns. That evaluative step separates finance reasoning from mechanical formula use.

Seminar angle

Seminar 7 is the best source for tightening this topic because it pushes from simple DDM logic into P/E interpretation and growth-opportunity valuation.

Common mistake

Students often memorise the formula or definition but fail to explain what changes value, what changes risk, and which timing convention the problem is using. In finance, those details usually determine the correct answer.
