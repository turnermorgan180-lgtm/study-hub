Question 1

No-growth P/E

Full seminar question

No-Growth Industries pays out all of its earnings as dividends. It will pay its next $4 per share dividend in 1 year. The discount rate is 12%. What is the price-earnings ratio of the company? What would the P/E ratio be if the discount rate was 10%?

Model answerSeminar

If all earnings are paid as dividends and growth is zero, price is D1 / r. With D1 = 4 and r = 12 percent, price is 33.33 and the P/E ratio is 8.33.

If the discount rate falls to 10 percent, price rises to 40 and the P/E ratio rises to 10.

Question 2

Plowback and P/E

Full seminar question

Stormy Weather has no attractive investment opportunities. Its return on equity equals the discount rate of 10%. Its expected earnings next year are $4 per share. Find the stock price, P/E ratio, and growth rate of dividends for plowback ratios of zero, 40%, and 80%.

Model answerSeminar

Because return on equity equals the discount rate at 10 percent, reinvestment adds zero net present value. Price stays at 40 and P/E stays at 10 whether plowback is 0, 40, or 80 percent.

What changes is the split between dividends now and growth later, not total value.

Question 3

Implied discount rate

Full seminar question

A German company paid a dividend per share of €2 and EPS of €4.62 at the end of last year. The ROE of the firm was 11.85% and the share price at the start of last year was €99.63. What is the appropriate discount rate on the firm’s equity?

Model answerSeminar

Retention ratio is 1 - 2 / 4.62 = 56.71 percent. Growth is retention x ROE = 0.5671 x 11.85 percent = 6.72 percent.

Required return is D1 / P0 + g = 2 / 99.63 + 6.72 percent = about 8.73 percent.

Question 4

Multi-stage DDM

Full seminar question

The ROE of company X is 14% and its payout ratio is 0.5. Current EPS is £7 and this will grow as the firm reinvests earnings. Assume ROE and payout ratio stay constant for the next 4 years. In year 5, the dividend will be £4.387 and from year 5 ROE falls to 11.5% and the payout ratio increases to 0.8 as a perpetuity. The appropriate discount rate is 11.5%. What are the company’s EPS and dividends next year? What is the company’s share price?

Model answerSeminar

Next-year EPS is 7 x 1.07 = 7.49 because growth equals 14 percent x 50 percent = 7 percent. Next-year dividend is 7.49 x 0.5 = 3.745.

Discounting the explicit dividends and the year-four continuation value gives a current share price of about 43.49.

Question 5

PVGO example

Full seminar question

Firm Y expects to earn £43 million per year in perpetuity if it does not undertake any new investments. It can instead invest £31 million today and £33 million in one year in real estate, generating annual earnings of £36 million in perpetuity beginning 2 years from today. The firm has 10 million shares and the required return on equity is 10%. What is the share price with and without the project?

Model answerSeminar

Without the project, the firm is a cash cow worth 43 million / 0.10 = 430 million, or 43 per share.

The project has PVGO of about 266.27 million after accounting for the 31 million today, 33 million in one year, and the perpetuity from year two. That adds about 26.63 per share, so undertaking the project raises the share price to about 69.63.

Question 6

Later reinvestment

Full seminar question

The annual earnings of XYZ will be 5.5 Swedish kroner per share in perpetuity if the firm makes no new investments and pays out all earnings as dividends. Alternatively, 3 years from now and in every subsequent year the company can invest 25% of its earnings in new projects earning 40% at year-end in perpetuity. The discount rate is 14%. What is the share price with and without the new investment?

Model answerSeminar

Without new investment, price is 5.5 / 0.14 = 39.29 SEK.

With reinvestment from year three onward, growth is 25 percent x 40 percent = 10 percent. The positive PVGO lifts the price to about 88.41 SEK.

Question 7

Phoenix FCF

Full seminar question

Phoenix Corporation faltered during the Covid pandemic but is recovering. Free cash flow forecasts for 2022 to 2026 are 0, 1.0, 2.9, 3.2, and 3.5 million. Recovery is complete by 2026 and there will be no further growth. Calculate the PV of free cash flow at a cost of equity of 10%, the price per share if Phoenix has 12 million shares, and the expected rate of return on Phoenix stock in each year from 2022 to 2026.

Model answerSeminar

Discounting the explicit 2022 to 2026 free cash flows at 10 percent and adding a no-growth terminal value from 2026 gives total equity value of about 29.10 million.

With 12 million shares, price per share is about 2.42. If the stock is fairly priced each year, the expected return in each year is the 10 percent cost of equity.

Question 8

Mexican Motors

Full seminar question

Mexican Motors’ market cap is 200 billion pesos. Next year’s free cash flow is 9.1 billion pesos. Analysts forecast free cash flow will grow by 8.10% per year for the next five years. Assume first that 8.10% continues forever. What rate of return are investors expecting? If the company maintains ROE of 9% and reinvests 50% of earnings long run, what will be the growth rate of earnings and what would be the rate of return?

Model answerSeminar

If free cash flow of 9.1 on a market cap of 200 grows forever at 8.10 percent, expected return is FCF1 / P0 + g = 9.1 / 200 + 8.10 percent = 12.55 percent.

If long-run ROE is 9 percent and the reinvestment rate is 50 percent, long-run earnings growth is 4.5 percent. Holding the same free-cash-flow yield logic then implies a lower long-run expected return of about 9.05 percent.

Question 9

Preference shares

Full seminar question

Explain the advantages and disadvantages of preference shares.

Model answerSeminar

Advantages include priority over ordinary shares for dividends and liquidation claims, and relatively stable income if dividends are maintained.

Disadvantages include limited voting rights, fixed upside compared with common equity, and the fact that preference dividends are usually not tax deductible for the issuer.

Question 10

T plc framework

Full seminar question

T plc has 10 million outstanding shares. The company expects to generate from its current assets earnings in the coming year of £750,000 which can be maintained indefinitely. Potential new investments are available, and from year 2 every year half of net earnings will be devoted to new investments with an average expected return of about 16%. The market’s required return is 14%. What is the value of total equity?

Model answerSeminar

The visible source file does not preserve the full investment schedule clearly enough to produce a defensible exact valuation. A first-class approach would value earnings from current assets as a perpetuity, then add the present value of each new investment stream and the value created by the later policy of reinvesting half of earnings at 16 percent.
