Last-Day Revision Summary

A compressed view of what repeatedly matters most: discounting, bond yields, dividend models, PVGO, expected return, diversification and CAPM.

Exam pattern: the paper repeatedly rewards clean valuation setup, correct rate matching, and a short sentence interpreting what the number means for the investor or firm.
L1-L3

Corporate Finance Logic

  • The goal is shareholder value, not raw accounting profit.
  • Finance decisions compare cash flows by timing and risk, not just size.
  • Financial markets and intermediaries exist to move funds, price risk and improve allocation.
L2

Time Value of Money

  • Discount future cash flows when comparing values today.
  • Compounding frequency must match the payment pattern.
  • Annuity questions usually fail because students mix up PV and FV or use the wrong timing assumption.
L4-L5

Bonds

  • Bond price is the PV of coupons plus redemption value.
  • Price and yield move inversely.
  • Premium bond implies coupon rate above market yield; discount bond implies the reverse.
  • Convertible and short-term finance questions usually turn on option-like upside versus financing flexibility.
L6-L7

Equity

  • Share value is the PV of future cash paid to shareholders.
  • Use Gordon growth only when growth is stable and below the required return.
  • PVGO separates value from current earnings power and value from future growth opportunities.
L8-L9

Risk, Return and CAPM

  • Expected return is probability weighted; risk is about dispersion.
  • Diversification reduces firm-specific risk through imperfect correlation.
  • CAPM prices systematic risk only, so beta matters for diversified investors.
  • The Security Market Line links required return to beta, not to total variance.
L10

Personal Finance

  • Mortgage, lease and retirement questions are all TVM problems in disguise.
  • Always state the decision rule: choose the lower PV cost or higher PV benefit.
  • Personal-finance answers improve when you explain affordability, risk exposure and opportunity cost together.
Exam priorities

Likely High-Value Topics

  • Present and future value of cash flows
  • Bond pricing and yield
  • Dividend discount and Gordon growth
  • PVGO and no-growth comparison
  • Portfolio return, variance and beta
Technique

First-Class Method

  • Write the formula before numbers.
  • Use labels for each variable.
  • Keep units visible throughout.
  • Finish with one sentence of business meaning, not just arithmetic.
