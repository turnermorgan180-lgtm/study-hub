Question 1

Organisation choices

Full seminar question

Which form of business organisation might best suit the following?
• A consulting firm with several senior consultants and support staff.
• A house painting company owned and operated by a college student who hires some friends for occasional help.
• A manufacturing company with sales of $900 million and 5,000 employees.

Model answerSeminar

The consulting firm is best structured as a partnership or LLP because several senior professionals contribute human capital, want shared control, and usually need liability protection without the separation costs of a public corporation.

The student house-painting business is best treated as a sole proprietorship because the scale is small, ownership is concentrated, and the administrative burden needs to stay low.

The manufacturing company should be a corporation because very large scale, many employees, and heavy capital needs require limited liability, transferable ownership, and easier access to debt and equity markets.

Why this scores well

A first-class answer does not just name the structure. It links scale, control, liability, and financing needs to the recommended legal form.

Question 2

Budgeting vs financing

Full seminar question

Are the following capital budgeting or financing decisions?
• Intel decides to spend $500 million to develop a new microprocessor.
• Volkswagen decides to raise 350 million euros through a bank loan.
• ExxonMobil constructs a pipeline to bring natural gas onshore from the Gulf of Mexico.
• Novartis buys a license to produce and sell a new drug developed by a biotech company.
• Pfizer issues new shares to acquire a small biotech company.

Model answerSeminar

Intel developing a new microprocessor is a capital-budgeting decision because the firm is choosing a real investment project.

Volkswagen raising funds through a bank loan is a financing decision because it concerns how the firm obtains capital.

ExxonMobil building a pipeline and Novartis buying a production licence are capital-budgeting decisions because both commit funds to productive assets or rights.

Pfizer issuing new shares to acquire a biotech company is primarily a financing decision in the form stated, though the acquisition itself also has an investment dimension.

Question 3

Treasurer vs controller

Full seminar question

Fritz and Frieda went to business school together 10 years ago. They have just been hired by a midsized corporation that wants to bring in new financial managers. Fritz studied finance, with an emphasis on financial markets and institutions. Frieda majored in accounting and became a CPA 5 years ago. Who is more suited to be a treasurer and who controller? Briefly explain.

Model answerSeminar

Fritz is better suited to the treasurer role because treasury work deals with funding, financial markets, cash management, and banking relationships.

Frieda is better suited to the controller role because accounting, reporting, compliance, and internal control fit her CPA background much more closely.

Question 4

Bank loan as asset

Full seminar question

Why is a bank loan a financial asset?

Model answerSeminar

A bank loan is a financial asset because it gives the lender a contractual claim on future cash flows from the borrower.

From the bank’s side, the loan appears on the asset side of the balance sheet because it is expected to generate interest and principal repayments.

Question 5

Profit maximisation limits

Full seminar question

Highlight some problems associated with using profit maximization as the goal of firm.

Model answerSeminar

Profit maximisation is too narrow because it can ignore timing, risk, and the distinction between accounting profit and cash flow.

It can also encourage short-termism, underinvestment in reputation or ESG issues, and decisions that raise reported profit while reducing long-run shareholder value.

Question 6

Payment choice

Full seminar question

Your company has just entered into a 12 months contract to supply equipment to staff. There are 2 payment options, the 1st is to pay £50,000 every month for 12 months and the 2nd to pay £600,000 at the end of the year. Which option should you choose?

Model answerSeminar

The monthly-payment option should normally be preferred because paying later reduces the present value of the obligation when money has time value.

A rough comparison shows that paying GBP50,000 monthly creates a lower present value than paying GBP600,000 as a single amount at year-end if the discount rate is positive. The finance principle is simple: defer cash outflow when the nominal totals are the same.

Question 7

Triple bottom line

Full seminar question

In the lecture it is mentioned that firms should have corporate social responsibility. Read the book section on the Triple Bottom Line, explain what it is and then discuss if you think that the principles argued by the triple bottom line are in line with CSR and whether you consider it important for firms’ management.

Model answerSeminar

The triple bottom line expands performance assessment from profit alone to profit, people, and planet. A firm is judged not only by financial outcomes but also by social and environmental impact.

That is broadly consistent with CSR because both frameworks reject the idea that firms owe duties only to immediate owners. For management, it matters because regulatory risk, reputation, labour retention, and customer trust all affect long-run value.

Question 8

Real vs financial assets

Full seminar question

Which of the following are real assets and which are financial assets? Share of a company; a personal IOU; a trademark; a factory; undeveloped land; the balance of a firm’s bank account; an experienced and hardworking sales force; a bond.

Model answerSeminar

Financial assets: share of a company, personal IOU, bank balance, bond. These are claims on cash flows.

Real assets: trademark, factory, undeveloped land, experienced sales force. These are productive resources or intangible capabilities used to generate income.

Question 9

Agency problem

Full seminar question

At a board meeting in December 2012 the managing director of FineTune plc announced that the company’s directors have been awarded substantial cash bonuses and share options, despite the company incurring substantial losses during the last financial year. Is this an agency problem? Explain why. If it is an agency problem explain the ways in which it may be resolved.

Model answerSeminar

Yes, this is a classic agency problem if directors are rewarded heavily despite poor performance. Managers may be extracting private benefit while shareholders bear the loss.

Possible remedies include independent remuneration committees, stronger disclosure, bonus deferral, clawback clauses, performance-linked share awards, and shareholder oversight.

Question 10

Compensation alignment

Full seminar question

Discuss which of the following forms of compensation is most likely to align the interests of managers and shareholders: a fixed salary; a salary linked to company profits; a salary that is paid partly in the form of company shares.

Model answerSeminar

A fixed salary provides income security but weak alignment. A salary linked to profits is better, but profits can be managed and may still encourage short-term manipulation.

The strongest alignment usually comes from compensation partly paid in company shares, especially if the shares vest over time, because managers then bear more of the same upside and downside as shareholders.

Question 13

Implied rates

Full seminar question

Find the interest rate implied by the following combinations of present and future values: $400 for 11 years to $684; $300 for 7 years to $300.

Model answerSeminar

For 400 growing to 684 in 11 years, r = (684 / 400)^(1/11) - 1 = about 5.0 percent.

For 300 growing to 300 in 7 years, the implied rate is 0 percent.

Question 14

Growth time

Full seminar question

How long will it take for $400 to grow to $1,000 at the interest rate specified: a. 4% b. 16%?

Model answerSeminar

At 4 percent, t = ln(1000 / 400) / ln(1.04) = about 23.36 years.

At 16 percent, the same growth takes about 6.17 years.

Question 15

Doubling time

Full seminar question

If you earn 6% per year on your bank account, how long will it take an account with $100 to double to $200?

Model answerSeminar

t = ln(2) / ln(1.06) = about 11.90 years. This is close to the rule-of-72 shortcut of roughly 12 years.

Question 16

Account value

Full seminar question

Five years ago your mother opened a bank account in your name with an initial balance of £1000, at the end of year 1 she deposited an extra £500, then 2 years and a half later £1600. For the first 3 years the bank paid an annual interest rate of 2% and thereafter 1.5%. How much money do you have in the bank? What if at the end of year 4 you withdrew £600?

Model answerSeminar

This is a mixed-cash-flow compounding problem. The clean method is to compound each deposit forward to today using 2 percent for the first three years and 1.5 percent thereafter, then subtract any later withdrawal compounded or not compounded depending on timing.

The mark-scoring step is not guesswork but timeline discipline: place 1000 at time 0, 500 at the end of year 1, 1600 at year 3.5, then compound each amount to year 5. If GBP600 is withdrawn at the end of year 4, it must be subtracted before the final year of 1.5 percent growth.

Question 17

CEO pay discussion

Full seminar question

Critics have argued that compensation of executives particularly in the finance sector is simply too high. Look at the financial accounts of some banks in your region and determine total pay of their CEO. Are such amounts excessive? Explain.

Model answerSeminar

A first-class answer would compare CEO pay with bank size, profitability, risk taken, long-run shareholder returns, and stakeholder outcomes rather than relying on moral instinct alone.

Without a current firm-specific lookup, the defensible analytical conclusion is that very high pay is only justified if it reflects sustained value creation and is structured with deferral, performance hurdles, and clawback protection.
