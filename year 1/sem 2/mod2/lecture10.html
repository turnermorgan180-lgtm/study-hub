Core finance rule

Use present value to compare alternatives that involve different timing, different rates, or different repayment structures.

Part 01

Why Personal Finance Belongs in the Module

Personal finance uses the same logic as corporate finance: cash flows arrive over time, borrowing is costly, saving earns returns, and decisions should be compared on a value-consistent basis.

Part 02

Budgeting Across Time

Households smooth consumption over time by borrowing, saving, insuring, or investing. That means time value of money is not only a corporate tool; it is a practical way of comparing financial choices across life stages.

Part 03

Credit, Saving and Financial Planning

A strong answer links financial planning to interest rates, liquidity needs, and risk tolerance. Good decisions depend not only on maximising return but on matching the product to the objective.

Part 04

Present Value in Everyday Decisions

The same logic used for bond pricing and annuities can be applied to mortgages, loans, pension saving, or instalment plans. The stronger answer makes that connection explicit.

Part 05

Risk and Household Decisions

Individuals also face investment risk, inflation risk, and liquidity risk. Personal finance therefore involves choosing not just how much to save or borrow, but where to place resources and how much uncertainty to accept.

Part 06

Module Integration

This final topic is useful because it brings the module full circle: finance fundamentals are not just formulas for markets, but a general framework for making intertemporal decisions well.

Seminar angle

The spreadsheet practice files are useful here because personal finance often becomes clearer when cash-flow comparisons are laid out period by period.

Common mistake

Students often memorise the formula or definition but fail to explain what changes value, what changes risk, and which timing convention the problem is using. In finance, those details usually determine the correct answer.
