Seminar focus

Correlation and Regression.

Question 1

Meaning of correlation

Full seminar question

Correlation tells you that what?

Model answerSeminar

The defensible answer is that two variables are linked. Correlation does not prove causation, does not guarantee significance, and does not rule out hidden variables.

Question 2

Why graph first

Full seminar question

Why do we graph data before regressing it?

Model answerSeminar

Because we need to see whether a linear relationship is plausible and whether there are outliers, clusters, or curvature that would make a straight-line regression misleading.

Question 3

SPSS output truth

Full seminar question

Which statement is true about the SPSS output?

Model answerSeminar

A correct reading identifies the fitted equation, sample size, or significance result from the actual output rather than confusing R with the percentage of variation explained. Remember that R-squared, not R, is the proportion explained.

Question 4

Slope from three points

Full seminar question

Given the points (2.7, 9.7), (12.9, 9.8), and (17.3, 11.6), what is the slope B?

Model answerSeminar

Using the least-squares slope formula for the three points gives a slope of about 0.14.

Question 5

Predicted value

Full seminar question

Given the points (6.4, 2), (5, 6.1), and (17.3, 16.2), what is the predicted y value for x = 7.3?

Model answerSeminar

Estimate the least-squares regression line first, then substitute x = 7.3. The predicted y value is about 5.93.

Question 6

Interpolation caution

Full seminar question

Interpolation of data is predictive, and caution must be used. True or false?

Model answerSeminar

True. Interpolation is generally safer than extrapolation, but it is still a model-based prediction and should be treated carefully rather than as certainty.
