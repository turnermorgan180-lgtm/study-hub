Seminar focus

Hypothesis Tests.

Question 1

Critical value for proportion test

Full seminar question

Background reading suggests 27% of people have a particular characteristic. A sample of 119 people found 55%. If we are looking for a significant difference, what is the 5% critical value?

Model answerSeminar

For a two-tailed 5 percent test using the normal approximation, the critical value is 1.96.

Question 2

One-sample mean test statistic

Full seminar question

Theory estimates a value should be 61.3. A sample of 64 measurements has mean 70 and standard deviation 13.5. What is the test statistic?

Model answerSeminar

Standard error is 13.5 / sqrt(64) = 1.6875. The test statistic is (70 - 61.3) / 1.6875 = 5.16 approximately.

Question 3

One-sample proportion test statistic

Full seminar question

Background reading suggests 31% of people have a characteristic. A sample of 37 people found 74% instead. What is the test statistic?

Model answerSeminar

Using the null proportion in the standard error, z = (0.74 - 0.31) / sqrt[0.31(0.69)/37] = 5.65 approximately.

Question 4

Pooled standard deviation

Full seminar question

Two groups with sample sizes 17 and 13 have standard deviations of 10.9 and 5.1. What is the pooled standard deviation?

Model answerSeminar

Use the pooled variance formula. The pooled standard deviation is about 8.89.

Question 5

Critical value for paired improvement test

Full seminar question

Five before-and-after observations are given and improvement is being tested. What is the 5% critical value?

Model answerSeminar

With five paired observations, degrees of freedom are 4. For a one-tailed 5 percent paired t-test, the critical value is about 2.132.

Question 6

Paired test statistic

Full seminar question

Another five before-and-after sample is given. What is the test statistic for the paired data?

Model answerSeminar

Compute the differences first, then divide the mean difference by the standard error of the differences. The result for the given data is about 4.43, indicating strong evidence of improvement.

Question 7

Reject or not

Full seminar question

Can we reject the null hypotheses?

Model answerSeminar

Where the absolute test statistic exceeds the relevant critical value, reject the null. Where it does not, fail to reject. The proper answer must mention the test, significance level, and context rather than just saying yes or no.

Question 8

Difference in proportions

Full seminar question

Two groups of sizes 37 and 31 have percentages 59% and 66% with a characteristic. What is the test statistic for whether these proportions are significantly different?

Model answerSeminar

Using the pooled proportion approach, the test statistic is about 0.58. That is far below a typical 5 percent critical value, so there is no strong evidence of a difference.
