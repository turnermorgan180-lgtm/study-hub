Question 1

Median from frequency table

Full seminar question

Calculate the median of the frequency distribution for sales values 0 to 6 with frequencies 5, 16, 12, 6, 5, 2, and 1.

Model answerSeminar

Total frequency is 47, so the median is the 24th observation. Cumulative frequency reaches 21 at sales = 1 and 33 at sales = 2, so the 24th observation lies in the sales = 2 category. The median is therefore 2.

Question 2

Five-number summary from frequency table

Full seminar question

Calculate the five-number summary of the same sales frequency table.

Model answerSeminar

Expanding the ordered data gives minimum 0 and maximum 6. The median is 2. The lower quartile sits in the lower half at 1, and the upper quartile sits in the upper half at 3. A defensible five-number summary is therefore 0, 1, 2, 3, 6.

Question 3

Inter-quartile range

Full seminar question

Calculate the inter-quartile range of 1.79, 1.08, 1.51, 1.86, 1.28, 1.46, 1.38, 1.32, 1.09, 1.01, and 1.21.

Model answerSeminar

Ordering the data gives lower quartile 1.08 and upper quartile 1.51 using the common median-of-halves rule. The inter-quartile range is therefore 1.51 minus 1.08 = 0.43.

Question 4

Five-number summary of raw data

Full seminar question

Find the five-number summary of 18, 20, 17, 10, 19, 14, 14, 19, 15, 16 and 12.

Model answerSeminar

Ordered data are 10, 12, 14, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 19, 20. Minimum is 10, median is 16, lower quartile is 14, upper quartile is 19, and maximum is 20.

Question 5

Range

Full seminar question

Calculate the range of 18, 20, 17, 10, 19, 14, 14, 19, 15, 16 and 12.

Model answerSeminar

Range equals maximum minus minimum, so 20 minus 10 = 10.

Question 6

Standard deviation

Full seminar question

What is the standard deviation of 4, 93, 43, 93, 34?

Model answerSeminar

The mean is 53.4. Using the sample standard deviation, the result is about 38.75. If population standard deviation were requested instead, the answer would differ slightly, so you should always state which convention you are using.

Question 7

Standardising a value

Full seminar question

A dataset has mean 32 and standard deviation 9. Standardise the value 48.

Model answerSeminar

The z-score is (48 - 32) / 9 = 1.78 approximately. This means 48 lies about 1.78 standard deviations above the mean.

Question 8

Mean from frequency table

Full seminar question

Find the mean of x values 0, 8, 12, 18, 21, 27 with frequencies 9, 10, 25, 12, 10, 6.

Model answerSeminar

Compute the weighted total and divide by total frequency. The total is 1,128 and total frequency is 72, so the mean is 15.67 approximately.

Question 9

Boxplot interpretation

Full seminar question

Which statement is true of a boxplot?

Model answerSeminar

The correct answer is that a boxplot can demonstrate skew in the data. It does not give the mode directly, does not show standard deviation explicitly, and is not a time-trend graph.

Question 10

Coefficient of variation

Full seminar question

What is the coefficient of variation of 62, 51, 8, 86, 33?

Model answerSeminar

Using the sample standard deviation and dividing by the mean gives a coefficient of variation of about 0.59, or 59 percent. That indicates relatively high spread compared with the average level.

Question 11

Robust spread measure

Full seminar question

Which measure of spread is least affected by outliers?

Model answerSeminar

The inter-quartile range is least affected because it focuses on the middle 50 percent of the data rather than the full range or squared deviations from the mean.

Question 12

Shift and IQR

Full seminar question

If all datapoints decrease by 7, the inter-quartile range decreases by 7. True or false?

Model answerSeminar

False. Subtracting the same constant from every observation shifts the whole distribution but does not change the distance between quartiles, so the IQR stays the same.
