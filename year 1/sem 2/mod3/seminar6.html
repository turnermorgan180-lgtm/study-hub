Seminar focus

Chi-Squared.

Question 1

Types of chi-squared test

Full seminar question

What are the two types of chi-squared test?

Model answerSeminar

They are the goodness-of-fit test and the test of association. One compares observed counts with a claimed distribution, while the other examines whether two categorical variables are independent.

Question 2

Expected fair-die frequencies

Full seminar question

A six-sided die is thrown many times with frequencies 73, 74, 43, 63, 42, and 90. What frequency would be expected for each face if the die were fair?

Model answerSeminar

Total throws are 385. For a fair die each face should appear one-sixth of the time, so the expected count for each face is 385 / 6 = 64.17 approximately.

Question 3

Goodness-of-fit test statistic

Full seminar question

A national vote has 28% for and 72% against. Locally, 1,508 voters include 614 in favour. What is the chi-squared goodness-of-fit test statistic?

Model answerSeminar

Expected local counts under the national pattern are 422.24 in favour and 1,085.76 against. Comparing observed and expected gives a chi-squared statistic of about 120.56, which is extremely large.

Question 4

Degrees of freedom for association

Full seminar question

A chi-squared association table has 4 rows and 3 columns. Which row of the table gives the critical value?

Model answerSeminar

Degrees of freedom are (4 - 1)(3 - 1) = 6, so use the row for 6 degrees of freedom.

Question 5

Expected count in a cell

Full seminar question

A table of Age by Preferred Music is given. If variables were independent, what would be expected in the Metal and Young cell?

Model answerSeminar

Expected count equals row total x column total divided by the grand total. Using the given table, the Young row total is 126, the Metal column total is 112, and the grand total is 282, so the expected count is 50.04 approximately.

Question 6

Association test statistic

Full seminar question

A researcher studies Age and Drink Preference with a 2x2 table. What is the test statistic?

Model answerSeminar

First compute expected counts, then sum (O-E)^2/E across all four cells. For the given table, the chi-squared statistic is about 0.95.

Question 7

SPSS output interpretation

Full seminar question

Which interpretation of the SPSS output is correct?

Model answerSeminar

The correct interpretation is the one that links the p-value to the chosen significance level and states whether we reject or fail to reject the null of independence. A result such as p = 0.041 supports rejecting the null at the 5 percent level in a one-tailed or equivalent context.

Question 8

Expected counts rule

Full seminar question

Why does the expected-count rule matter in chi-squared testing?

Model answerSeminar

If expected counts are too small, the chi-squared approximation becomes unreliable. Strong answers therefore comment on validity, not just the statistic itself.
