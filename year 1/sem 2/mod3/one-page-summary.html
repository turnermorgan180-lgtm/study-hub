Quantitative Analysis in Business Studies · One-Page Summary

Last-Day Revision Summary

A compressed exam sheet for the methods that recur most often in the closed exam, mock, and SPSS practicals: probability, distributions, confidence intervals, tests, chi-squared, ANOVA, regression, and test selection in SPSS.

Exam pattern: strong answers usually score because the method choice is explicit, the calculation is set out cleanly, and the final line interprets the statistic rather than just naming it.
L1

Descriptive Statistics

  • Know mean, median, quartiles, range, IQR and standard deviation.
  • Boxplots show centre, spread and possible outliers quickly.
  • Always comment on shape or spread when asked to interpret.
L2-L3

Probability and Distributions

  • Use addition, multiplication and conditional probability rules carefully.
  • Bayes questions require a correct denominator, not just a remembered formula.
  • Binomial questions usually split into “exactly r” and complement-rule variants.
L4

Sampling and Confidence Intervals

  • Sampling variability falls as sample size rises.
  • Confidence intervals estimate plausible values for the population parameter.
  • A higher confidence level gives a wider interval.
L5

Hypothesis Testing

  • State null and alternative hypotheses first.
  • Choose the right tail structure before computing the test statistic.
  • Conclude in context: reject or fail to reject, then explain what that means for the business claim.
L6-L7

Chi-Squared and ANOVA

  • Chi-squared compares observed and expected frequencies.
  • ANOVA tests whether at least one group mean differs.
  • Both methods reward clear hypotheses and correct interpretation of p-values.
L8-L9

Regression and SPSS Workflow

  • Regression estimates the line of best fit and the marginal effect of x on y.
  • R-squared measures fit, not causality.
  • SPSS output is evidence; the answer still needs method choice and interpretation.
L10

SPSS Hypothesis Tests

  • Use One-Sample T Test for a continuous mean against a fixed value.
  • Use Paired-Samples T Test for two continuous measures on the same people.
  • Use Independent-Samples T Test or ANOVA for continuous outcomes across groups.
  • Use chi-squared methods for categorical frequency or association questions.
  • Read Sig. as the p-value and compare it with 0.05 before writing the contextual conclusion.
Exam priorities

Likely High-Value Topics

  • Conditional probability and Bayes
  • Binomial and normal calculations
  • Confidence intervals
  • Hypothesis testing
  • Chi-squared, ANOVA, regression and SPSS interpretation
Technique

First-Class Method

  • Name the method before the formula.
  • Show full workings to two decimal places where relevant.
  • State assumptions when the method depends on them.
  • End with one plain-English conclusion tied to the question.
