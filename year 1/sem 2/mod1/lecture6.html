Revenue, Profit and Advertising

Product decisions, total, average and marginal revenue, accounting versus economic profit, profit maximisation, advertising strategy, and how non-price competition changes demand conditions.

Key Definitions

Terms to Know Cold

Total revenue
Price multiplied by quantity sold.
Average revenue
Revenue per unit sold. In most models it is the price.
Marginal revenue
The extra revenue from selling one more unit.
Accounting profit
Revenue minus explicit monetary costs.
Economic profit
Revenue minus explicit and implicit opportunity costs.
Persuasive advertising
Promotion that shifts preferences and differentiates the product.
Part 01

Revenue Concepts and the Shape of Demand

Total revenue rises when a firm sells more output, but the rate at which it rises depends on market structure. In perfect competition the firm is a price taker, so each extra unit is sold at the same market price and AR = MR = price. In imperfect competition the firm faces a downward-sloping demand curve, so selling more usually requires a lower price and MR falls below AR.

Core rule

If demand slopes downward, marginal revenue lies below average revenue. The firm gains revenue from the extra unit, but loses some revenue on previous units because price falls.

Perfect competitionHorizontal at market priceEqual to price
Monopoly or monopolistic competitionDownward-sloping demand curveBelow average revenue

Part 02

Profit Means More Than Accounting Surplus

Business economics distinguishes between accounting profit and economic profit because firms must cover not only cash expenses but also the opportunity cost of owner time, retained capital, and alternative uses of resources. A business can look profitable in accounting terms while still earning less than its resources could earn elsewhere.

The stronger exam answer therefore defines profit as a residual after relevant costs, not simply money left over after paying bills.

Common mistake

Do not say a firm should continue operating just because accounting profit is positive. The correct question is whether revenue covers variable cost in the short run and total economic cost in the long run.

Part 03

The Profit-Maximising Rule

The central business rule is simple: profit is maximised at the output where marginal revenue equals marginal cost, provided marginal cost is rising through that point. The intuition is that output should be expanded while the extra revenue from one more unit exceeds the extra cost of making it.

1
Compare MR and MC. If MR is greater, extra output adds to profit.
2
Stop where MR = MC. That is the last unit worth producing.
3
Read price from demand or AR. In imperfect competition, price is found above MR.
In diagram terms
  • Draw downward-sloping AR and steeper MR below it.
  • Draw U-shaped MC cutting MR at the chosen output.
  • Move vertically to AR to find the selling price.
  • Profit is the rectangle between price and average cost at that output if price exceeds AC.
Part 04

Advertising as a Demand-Side Decision

Advertising is treated here as an economic choice, not just a branding exercise. Informative advertising can make consumers aware of price, availability, and characteristics. Persuasive advertising can increase willingness to buy, shift demand outward, and weaken the closeness of substitutes.

If advertising makes demand stronger, total revenue may increase at a given price. If it also makes demand less elastic, the firm may gain more pricing power. That is why advertising and product differentiation are central in monopolistic competition and oligopoly.

Exam insight

The stronger answer explains how advertising works: demand may shift right, become less price elastic, or both. Do not stop at “advertising increases sales.”

Part 05

Branding, Product Differentiation and Elasticity

When firms create brand loyalty, consumers see rival products as less perfect substitutes. That reduces the price elasticity of demand faced by the firm. A lower elasticity means a price rise causes a smaller proportionate fall in quantity demanded, which can support higher margins.

Why that matters

  • It can raise average revenue at the chosen output.
  • It can protect market share against aggressive rivals.
  • It can make promotional spending rational even when production costs are unchanged.
Part 06

When Does Advertising Pay?

Advertising should be expanded up to the point where the marginal benefit of extra promotion equals the marginal cost of that promotion. The firm compares the extra contribution to revenue or profit with the cost of the campaign itself.

Part 07

Evaluation and Common Pitfalls

Advertising is not automatically welfare-improving. Informative advertising can reduce search costs and increase competition, but persuasive advertising may create artificial differentiation and barrier effects that protect market power. The correct evaluation depends on whether promotion improves information, expands consumer choice, or simply manipulates demand.

Common mistakes

Do not confuse sales maximisation with profit maximisation. A campaign that raises revenue can still lower profit if the advertising spend is too high.

Do not describe MR = MC as a condition for revenue maximisation. Revenue is maximised where MR = 0, not where MR = MC.

Part 08

Exam Practice

Section B Style Prompt12 marks

Explain how advertising can increase a firm's profit. Use revenue concepts and market structure logic in your answer.

Model structure

Start by defining total, average, and marginal revenue. Then explain that in imperfect competition the firm faces downward-sloping demand and can use advertising to shift demand right or reduce elasticity. Link that to higher AR, improved MR at relevant output levels, and potentially higher profit if the extra revenue exceeds the extra advertising cost. Finish with evaluation: advertising can also become an arms race, and informative and persuasive advertising have different welfare implications.
