Key Definitions

Terms to Know Cold

Demand
The quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at each possible price over a given period.
Supply
The quantity firms are willing and able to sell at each possible price over a given period.
Equilibrium
The price and quantity where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
Shortage
A situation where quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price.
Surplus
A situation where quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at the current price.
Price mechanism
The adjustment process through which prices respond to shortages and surpluses and move the market toward equilibrium.
Change in quantity demanded / supplied
A movement along the same curve caused only by a change in the good’s own price.
Change in demand / supply
A shift of the whole curve caused by a non-price determinant such as income or production costs.
01

What Makes a Market Competitive

The lecture starts from a simple free-market idea: consumers are free to make demand decisions and firms are free to make supply decisions. No central planner tells buyers what to purchase or producers what to make. Instead, prices coordinate their actions.

Core logic

In a competitive market, price acts as the communication signal between consumers and firms. When demand and supply do not match, price changes transmit that information and incentivise adjustment.

This is why competitive-market analysis matters so much. It is the benchmark case for later lectures. Government intervention, monopoly, taxation, deadweight loss, and welfare analysis all make more sense once you understand how a decentralised market would work without those distortions.

Exam insight

Do not define a competitive market vaguely as “a market with many firms”. For this lecture, the mark-scoring emphasis is the adjustment process: free decisions by buyers and sellers are reconciled through price changes.

02

Demand and the Demand Curve

Demand shows the relationship between price and quantity demanded. Holding all else constant, a lower price usually leads to a greater quantity demanded, while a higher price reduces quantity demanded. That gives the demand curve its downward slope.

What the curve means

A point on the demand curve shows how much consumers would buy at one particular price. The whole curve shows planned purchases across many possible prices.

How to describe the diagram
  • Price on the vertical axis, quantity on the horizontal axis.
  • Demand curve slopes downward from left to right.
  • A fall in price causes a movement along the demand curve.
  • A non-price change such as income or tastes causes the whole curve to shift.

The lecture PDF explicitly identifies demand as one half of the equilibrium story. If demand rises while supply is unchanged, the immediate effect is a shortage at the old price. That is the starting point for the price mechanism.

Common mistake

Students often write “demand increases because price falls.” That is incorrect terminology. A fall in price causes an increase in quantity demanded, not an increase in demand. Demand itself changes only if a non-price determinant changes.

03

Supply and the Supply Curve

Supply shows the relationship between price and quantity supplied. In general, a higher price makes production more worthwhile, so firms offer more for sale. That gives the supply curve its upward slope.

What shifts supply

The extracted lecture material specifically highlights costs of production and changes in input availability. Lower costs allow firms to supply more at every price, shifting supply to the right. Higher costs shift supply to the left.

Case What changes Graph effect Market meaning
Price rises Own price only Movement up along S Increase in quantity supplied
Costs fall Non-price determinant S shifts right Increase in supply
Costs rise Non-price determinant S shifts left Decrease in supply
Source-derived distinction

Change in quantity supplied means movement along the existing supply curve because the product’s price changes. Change in supply means a shift of the entire curve because some other determinant changes.

The source slide uses a potatoes example for the supply curve. The good itself is not important. The exam logic is. If production becomes cheaper, more is supplied at every price.

04

The Price Mechanism

Price is the coordinating device in a competitive market. The lecture states that prices act as the communication tool between firms and consumers by responding to shortages and surpluses.

1
Shortage appears: quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price.
2
Price is bid up: buyers compete for limited output and firms see a profit incentive to expand supply.
3
Quantity demanded contracts: some consumers are priced out or reduce purchases.
4
Quantity supplied expands: sellers produce and bring more to market.
5
Equilibrium is restored: the shortage disappears once demand and supply meet.

The same mechanism works in reverse for surplus. If price is above equilibrium, unsold stock accumulates. Sellers lower price to clear inventories. Quantity demanded rises and quantity supplied falls until the surplus disappears.

The price mechanism is not just “price changes”. It is a full adjustment story: imbalance → incentive to change price → movement along curves → restoration of equilibrium.
05

Equilibrium, Shortage, and Surplus

Equilibrium is the point where the plans of consumers and producers meet. The source PDF explicitly defines it this way and gives an illustrative example in which the equilibrium price is 60p, with 20p representing a shortage price and 100p a surplus price.

Definition

Equilibrium is the market-clearing price and quantity where Qd = Qs. At that price there is no tendency for price to change, provided the demand and supply curves do not shift.

Market position Relationship What happens next
Price below equilibrium Qd > Qs Shortage pushes price upward
At equilibrium Qd = Qs No pressure for price to change
Price above equilibrium Qs > Qd Surplus pushes price downward
Exam phrasing for the graph
  • Equilibrium is found at the intersection of the demand and supply curves.
  • If the market price is below equilibrium, there is excess demand.
  • If the market price is above equilibrium, there is excess supply.
  • The equilibrium price is the market-clearing price because it eliminates shortage and surplus.
06

Demand Shifts and the New Equilibrium

The extracted slide text gives a direct example: consumer incomes rise, so the demand curve shifts right. At the original price, quantity demanded now exceeds quantity supplied, creating a shortage.

1
Demand shifts right: consumers want more at every price.
2
Old price becomes too low: the market experiences excess demand.
3
Price rises: the shortage bids price upward.
4
Move along supply: firms expand quantity supplied in response to the higher price.
5
New equilibrium: both equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity rise.

This is an important comparative-statics result. A rightward demand shift causes a movement along the supply curve to a new intersection. The supply curve itself does not move unless a supply determinant changes.

Exam shortcut

If demand increases and supply is unchanged, the safe answer is: equilibrium price rises and equilibrium quantity rises. Then explain why: a shortage at the initial price triggers upward price adjustment.

07

Supply Shifts and the New Equilibrium

The source also gives the reverse-side example: production costs rise, shifting supply left. At the old price firms no longer want to supply as much, so a shortage appears.

1
Supply shifts left: less is supplied at every price because costs are higher.
2
Old price is unsustainable: quantity demanded now exceeds quantity supplied.
3
Price rises: the market rations demand and encourages remaining producers to supply more.
4
Move along demand: consumers reduce purchases as the price increases.
5
New equilibrium: equilibrium price rises but equilibrium quantity falls.

This result is examined frequently because it requires precision. If supply falls, price does not fall. Students who memorise “left shift means less” but forget the shortage logic often get the sign on price wrong.

Common mistake

Do not say “supply shifts left so firms sell less and price falls.” If supply shifts left while demand is unchanged, the correct result is higher equilibrium price and lower equilibrium quantity.

08

Combined Shifts and Exam Logic

If both curves shift, the new equilibrium is still just the intersection of the two new curves. The difficulty is not the graphing. It is knowing which variable can be signed unambiguously.

Shock combination Price effect Quantity effect
Demand rises, supply unchanged Rises Rises
Demand falls, supply unchanged Falls Falls
Supply rises, demand unchanged Falls Rises
Supply falls, demand unchanged Rises Falls
Demand rises and supply rises Ambiguous Rises
Demand falls and supply falls Ambiguous Falls

When both curves shift in opposite directions, one variable is often certain and the other ambiguous. Strong exam answers explicitly say “the overall effect on price is indeterminate without knowing the relative size of the shifts” rather than guessing.

09

Exam Questions and Model Answers

Section A Style 6 marks

Explain how a competitive market moves from shortage to equilibrium.

Model answer

A shortage exists when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price. Because buyers compete for limited output, price is bid up. The higher price reduces quantity demanded and increases quantity supplied, creating movements along the demand and supply curves. This process continues until quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. The market has then reached equilibrium, where there is no further pressure for price to change.

Section A Style 8 marks

Using a demand and supply diagram, explain the effect of a rise in consumer incomes on the market for a normal good.

Model answer

A rise in consumer incomes increases demand for a normal good, so the demand curve shifts right. At the original equilibrium price, quantity demanded now exceeds quantity supplied, creating a shortage. This shortage puts upward pressure on price. As price rises, there is a movement along the supply curve and firms increase quantity supplied. The new equilibrium therefore has a higher price and a higher quantity than before.

Section A Style 8 marks

Explain the difference between a change in supply and a change in quantity supplied.

Model answer

A change in quantity supplied is a movement along the same supply curve caused only by a change in the good’s own price. A change in supply is a shift of the entire supply curve caused by a non-price determinant, such as production costs, technology, or input availability. This distinction matters because the first leaves the curve in place, while the second changes the market equilibrium itself.

10

Evaluation and Common Mistakes

What strong answers do

  • Separate movements along curves from curve shifts.
  • State clearly whether the market starts in shortage or surplus.
  • Explain the full adjustment path, not just the final outcome.
  • Use equilibrium language precisely: market-clearing price, Qd = Qs, new intersection.
Frequent errors

Confusing “demand rises” with “quantity demanded rises”; assuming every leftward shift lowers price; forgetting that shortage pushes price up; and describing equilibrium as “where firms make most profit” rather than where planned demand equals planned supply.

Bigger picture

The competitive-market model is powerful because it shows how decentralised choices can generate coordinated outcomes. But it is still a benchmark. Later lectures add externalities, market power, and government intervention precisely because real markets do not always adjust cleanly or efficiently.
